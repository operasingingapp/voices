# Check 4: story, sections, arcs, WHEN lines, cast (app-boheme.md against the Ricordi vocal score)

Method: I read the full draft and checked every claim against the score. I used the page map to find my way and read the page images for each point listed below. Most of the file is accurate: the order of events, who sings what, objects (red-sea painting, broken mirror, Castoro, key, bonnet, muff, earrings, fire irons, herring, Musetta's cape), places and times all match the printed words and stage directions. The problems below are small.

## Problems

1. **S6 (Che gelida manina), "what happens"**
   - Draft: "it is no use searching in the dark, and the moon will soon be here."
   - Score, p. 64 sys 4 – p. 65 sys 1: "Ma per fortuna è una notte di luna, e qui la luna l'abbiamo vicina". This means "luckily it is a moonlit night, and here the moon is close to us". Nothing says the moon will arrive later.
   - Fix: "...it is no use searching in the dark, but luckily there is a moon, and here it is close by."

2. **S10 (Aranci, datteri!), "what happens"**
   - Draft: "promises her a coral necklace when his millionaire uncle dies."
   - Score, p. 96: Mimì admires "Bel vezzo di corallo!". Rodolfo answers: "Ho uno zio milionario. Se fa senno il buon Dio voglio comprarti un vezzo assai più bel!" He promises a much finer necklace, not the coral one. "When his uncle dies" is a fair reading of "if the good Lord sees sense", but it is an interpretation.
   - Fix: "Mimì admires a coral necklace; Rodolfo promises her a far finer one if the good Lord sees sense and his millionaire uncle dies."

3. **Act 1 summary and S2, "Schaunard locks the food away"**
   - Score, p. 30 (top direction): "poi leva le vivande dal tavolo e le mette nel piccolo armadio". He puts the food in the little cupboard. Nothing is locked. The only locking in this scene is Rodolfo locking the door ("chiude la porta a chiave", p. 32). Saying "locks" in both places muddles the two.
   - Fix: "puts the food away in the cupboard" in both places.

4. **A-marcello-4**
   - Draft: "He sells Musetta's earrings for medicine and fetches the doctor".
   - Score, p. 271: "Ho veduto il dottore! Verrà; gli ho fatto fretta. Ecco il cordial". Marcello has seen the doctor and hurried him, but the doctor has not come. S29 and S30 correctly say the doctor is on his way.
   - Fix: "...sells Musetta's earrings, brings back medicine and sends for the doctor".

5. **A-colline-1**
   - Draft: "comes in frozen and empty-handed (no pawnshop takes pledges on Christmas Eve)".
   - Score, p. 13: Colline enters "gettando con ira sul tavolo un pacco di libri legato con un fazzoletto". He comes back with the books he could not pawn, so "empty-handed" is literally wrong.
   - Fix: "comes in frozen, angrily throwing down the bundle of books he could not pawn (no pledges are taken on Christmas Eve)".

6. **A-benoit-1, unclear English**
   - Draft: "throw him out without paying."
   - This can be read as Benoit not paying. The score (pp. 38, 48) shows Marcello showing him the coins, and the tenants keep the rent.
   - Fix: "...and throw him out with the rent still unpaid."

7. **S22, unclear English**
   - Draft: "while Rodolfo and Mimì walk away together until the flowers come."
   - The phrase is awkward and nearly says the opposite of what they agree. Score, pp. 221–222: "Ci lascierem alla stagion dei fior", meaning they will part when the flowers come.
   - Fix: "while Rodolfo and Mimì walk away together, agreeing to part in the season of flowers."

8. **Act 1 summary, Mimì's work**
   - Draft: "she embroiders silk flowers".
   - Score, p. 70: "A tela o a seta ricamo in casa e fuori ... far gigli e rose", meaning she embroiders on linen or silk and makes lilies and roses. CAST says "on linen and silk", so the two parts of the file disagree.
   - Fix: "she embroiders flowers on linen and silk".

9. **A-mimi-3**
   - Draft: "telling him to keep the bonnet".
   - Score, p. 209: "Se vuoi, se vuoi serbarla a ricordo d'amor", meaning "if you want to keep it". The Act 3 summary and S21 correctly say "if he likes". This line overstates it and disagrees with them.
   - Fix: "telling him he may keep the bonnet if he likes".

## Unsure
- CAST, Benoit and Alcindoro, "(Sometimes sung by a tenor...)": the score has nothing on this. It is a claim about performance practice. Doubling the two roles is common, but a tenor Benoit or Alcindoro is unusual. Consider cutting "sometimes sung by a tenor".
- S3 / Act 1, "Flattered and tipsy" (Benoit): the score keeps refilling his glass (p. 39), but the only character marked "mezzo brillo" is Schaunard (p. 40). Calling Benoit tipsy is a reasonable inference, not printed.
- S21, "her little gold ring": "quel cerchietto d'or" (p. 208) could be a ring or a bracelet. Castel's literal translation would settle it.
- "rich viscount" (Act 4 summary, A-mimi-4): the score says only "fuggita dal Viscontino" (p. 249). Wealth is implied, not stated. The same goes for "now kept by the elderly Alcindoro" in STORY.
- A-schaunard-1, "then leads the way to the Café Momus": Schaunard proposes Momus (p. 49), but Rodolfo lights the others out with the lamp and nobody is shown leading (p. 52). "proposes the Café Momus" would be safer.
