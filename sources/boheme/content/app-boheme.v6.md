# STORY (shown on the opera's page)
Paris, the Latin Quarter. Four penniless friends share a freezing garret: Rodolfo the poet, Marcello the painter, Schaunard the musician and Colline the philosopher. On Christmas Eve Rodolfo stays behind to finish an article, and his neighbour Mimì knocks to ask for a light; before the night is out they are in love. At the Café Momus that night Marcello's old flame Musetta, who arrives with the pompous old Alcindoro, wins Marcello back and leaves Alcindoro to pay the bill. By February Rodolfo's jealousy hides a worse fear: Mimì is gravely ill and he is too poor to care for her. They agree to part when the spring comes. Some time later, back in the garret, Musetta brings Mimì in, dying; she wanted to be with Rodolfo at the end, and she dies there among the friends.

## Act 1
where: A garret in the Latin Quarter, Christmas Eve
- Rodolfo and Marcello are too cold to work: Marcello's hands are numb over his painting of the Red Sea, and the fireplace is cold. Rodolfo feeds it his play, first one act, then another, then the rest at once, while the philosopher Colline, back from a pawnshop that takes no pledges on Christmas Eve, joins them at the fire. Then two shop boys arrive with wood, food, wine and cigars, and Schaunard the musician throws down silver coins: an Englishman hired him to play until a parrot died, and he hurried things along with a sprig of parsley. Nobody listens to the story. Schaunard puts the food away in the cupboard for leaner days: on Christmas Eve they will drink at home and dine out.
- The landlord Benoit knocks for the rent. The friends pour him wine, get him boasting of a flirtation at the Mabille dance hall, and then throw him out in mock outrage at a married man's morals. The others leave for the Café Momus; Rodolfo stays five minutes to finish an article.
- Their neighbour Mimì knocks: her candle has gone out. Breathless from the stairs, she faints, comes round, leaves, and comes back for the key she has dropped; both candles blow out, and in the dark Rodolfo finds the key and pockets it. Their hands meet. He tells her he is a poet (“Che gelida manina”); she tells him she embroiders flowers on linen and silk and lives alone under the roofs (“Si. Mi chiamano Mimì”). The friends shout up from the courtyard; in the moonlight Rodolfo and Mimì declare their love (“O soave fanciulla”) and go out arm in arm to join them.

## Act 2
where: The Latin Quarter and the Café Momus, the same evening
- The square is packed with shoppers, street sellers and children. Schaunard buys a horn and a pipe, Colline buys a second-hand coat, has the mender stitch it, and finds a rare book, and Rodolfo buys Mimì a pink bonnet. At a table outside the Café Momus Rodolfo introduces her to his friends; the toy seller Parpignol passes with a cart of toys and a crowd of children behind him, and the friends order supper.
- Musetta arrives with Alcindoro, a pompous old gentleman, and does all she can to make Marcello look at her, until she sings a waltz straight at him about the effect she has on men (“Quando me'n vo'”). Pretending her shoe pinches, she sends Alcindoro off to buy her another pair and falls into Marcello's arms.
- The bill comes and the friends' pockets are empty. As a military tattoo approaches, Musetta has the two bills put together for the absent Alcindoro to pay; Marcello and Colline carry her, one shoe off, behind the band, and Alcindoro comes back with the new shoes to find only the bill.

## Act 3
where: A toll gate on the edge of Paris, a snowy dawn at the end of February
- Customs men let in street sweepers, milk women and farm women; from the tavern by the gate come drinking songs and Musetta's voice. Mimì arrives coughing and sends for Marcello, who now lives at the tavern with Musetta at the innkeeper's expense: she teaches the travellers singing and he paints soldiers on the front of the inn. She cannot go in: Rodolfo, asleep inside, is so jealous that they cannot go on together, and she begs Marcello to help them part.
- Rodolfo comes out, and she hides. He tells Marcello he wants to leave Mimì because she is a flirt; pressed, he admits the truth: she is gravely ill, his cold room is making her worse, and love is not enough to save her. Her coughing gives her away. Marcello runs back in when he hears Musetta laughing inside.
- Mimì says goodbye without bitterness and asks Rodolfo to gather up her few things for the porter to collect, but to keep the bonnet if he likes (“Donde lieta uscì”). Remembering what they will lose, they decide to stay together until the spring, while Marcello and Musetta come out quarrelling, trade insults and part.

## Act 4
where: The garret, some time later
- Rodolfo and Marcello pretend to work. Rodolfo says he has seen Musetta riding in a carriage, Marcello that he has seen Mimì, and each pretends not to care; then they give up and think of the women they have lost (“O Mimì tu più non torni”). Schaunard and Colline bring bread and a herring, and the four turn supper into a mock banquet, a dance and a duel with the fire irons.
- Musetta bursts in: Mimì is on the stairs, too weak to climb them. She has run away from her viscount and wanted to die near Rodolfo. There is nothing in the house; Musetta gives Marcello her earrings to sell for medicine and a doctor, and goes with him to buy Mimì a muff for her cold hands. Colline says goodbye to his old coat before taking it to the pawnshop (“Vecchia zimarra”), and takes Schaunard with him to leave the lovers alone.
- Mimì and Rodolfo remember the night they met (“Sono andati?”). The others come back; Mimì takes the muff, thinking it is from Rodolfo, and drifts off to sleep while Musetta prays. Schaunard sees that she has died; Rodolfo is the last to understand.

# CAST (name, voice type, one-line description)
The score prints no list of characters (the first page is the opening of Quadro Primo), so the voice types below come from the clefs and ranges in this score and from standard practice.
- [C-rodolfo] Rodolfo (Tenor): A poet, who shares the garret with his three friends.
- [C-mimi] Mimì (Soprano): Rodolfo's neighbour, who embroiders flowers on linen and silk for a living; her real name is Lucia.
- [C-marcello] Marcello (Baritone): A painter, Rodolfo's closest friend, still in love with Musetta.
- [C-musetta] Musetta (Soprano): Marcello's old flame, a flirt who still loves him.
- [C-schaunard] Schaunard (Baritone): A musician, the friend who comes home with money.
- [C-colline] Colline (Bass): A philosopher, the friend with a coat full of books.
- [C-benoit] Benoit (Bass): The friends' landlord, who comes for the rent.
- [C-alcindoro] Alcindoro (Bass): Musetta's pompous elderly admirer.
- [C-parpignol] Parpignol (Tenor): A toy seller who wheels his cart through the Latin Quarter on Christmas Eve.
- [C-sergente] Sergente (Bass; his few low notes would suit a bass or a baritone): The sergeant of the customs post at the toll gate.
- [C-doganiere] Doganiere (Bass; his few low notes would suit a bass or a baritone): A customs officer at the toll gate.

Small solo lines (not app characters): a street seller crossing the square in Act 2 (“Un venditore ambulante”, tenor: “Prugne di Tours!”); a boy in the children's chorus in Act 2 (“Ragazzo solo”: “Vo' la tromba, il cavallin!”).

Chorus groups (not app characters):
- Act 2: the street sellers (Venditori); the crowd of students, working girls, townsfolk and shopkeepers (La folla: Studenti, Sartine, Borghesi, Bottegaie); some women sellers (Alcune venditrici); urchins (Monelli); customers calling from the café (Dal Caffè); the children round Parpignol's cart (Bambine e ragazzi) and their mothers (Le mamme).
- Act 3: the street sweepers at the gate (Spazzaturai); women and men singing inside the tavern; the milk women (Lattivendole); the carters (Carrettieri); the farm women (Paesane).

# CHARACTER ARCS (act by act)
[A-rodolfo-1] Rodolfo · Act 1: Freezing in the garret, he burns his own play for warmth, drinks the wine Schaunard's money buys, joins in teasing the landlord, then stays behind to write. His neighbour Mimì knocks for a light; he pockets her lost key so as to go on searching with her in the dark, tells her who he is (“Che gelida manina”), and they fall in love in the moonlight (“O soave fanciulla”).
[A-rodolfo-2] Rodolfo · Act 2: He buys Mimì a pink bonnet in the Christmas crowd, is a little jealous when she looks at the students, and presents her proudly to his friends at the Café Momus. Watching Musetta's antics, he tells Mimì he would never forgive such behaviour, and at the end he marches off with her behind the band.
[A-rodolfo-3] Rodolfo · Act 3: After walking out on Mimì in the night, he tells Marcello at the toll gate that he wants to leave her because she is a flirt; pressed, he admits the truth: she is dying, and his poverty is killing her. Discovering that she has heard, he tries to take it back; she says goodbye, and they agree to stay together until the spring.
[A-rodolfo-4] Rodolfo · Act 4: Alone again, he pretends not to care that Mimì has been seen riding in a carriage dressed like a queen, then broods over the bonnet she left (“O Mimì tu più non torni”). When Musetta brings Mimì back dying, he lays her on his bed; alone with her he gives back the bonnet and remembers how they met (“Sono andati?”). He is the last to realise that she has died.
[A-mimi-1] Mimì · Act 1: Her candle out, she knocks at her neighbour's door, faints, and comes back for the key she has dropped. In the dark the poet takes her hand; she tells him she embroiders flowers and lives alone under the roofs (“Si. Mi chiamano Mimì”), and in the moonlight she falls in love (“O soave fanciulla”) and goes out with him to meet his friends.
[A-mimi-2] Mimì · Act 2: Rodolfo buys her a pink bonnet and introduces her to his friends at the Café Momus. She sees at once that Musetta is still in love with Marcello and pities her, and tells Rodolfo she loves him and is all his.
[A-mimi-3] Mimì · Act 3: Coughing, she finds Marcello at the toll gate and begs him to help her and Rodolfo part, because his jealousy makes their life impossible. Hidden, she hears Rodolfo confess that she is dying. She says goodbye without bitterness, telling him he may keep the bonnet if he likes (“Donde lieta uscì”), but they decide to stay together until the spring.
[A-mimi-4] Mimì · Act 4: Dying, she has run away from her viscount; Musetta finds her in the street and, at her wish, brings her back to Rodolfo's garret. Left alone with him, she remembers their first night (“Sono andati?”). When the others return she takes Musetta's muff, believing it is Rodolfo's gift, and falls asleep for the last time.
[A-marcello-1] Marcello · Act 1: The painter is too cold to paint his Red Sea, jokes about Musetta's icy heart, leads the teasing of the landlord, and goes ahead to the Café Momus, calling back up that Rodolfo has found poetry.
[A-marcello-2] Marcello · Act 2: Bitter about love, he sees Musetta arrive with an old admirer and pretends not to care, describing her to Mimì as a bird of prey that feeds on hearts. Her waltz (“Quando me'n vo'”) is aimed at him and he cannot resist; once she has sent the old man away he takes her in his arms and carries her off behind the band.
[A-marcello-3] Marcello · Act 3: Now living at the tavern by the toll gate with Musetta, he hears Mimì's despair over Rodolfo's jealousy, then Rodolfo's confession that she is dying. Catching Musetta flirting with a customer, he quarrels with her and they part with insults.
[A-marcello-4] Marcello · Act 4: Missing Musetta, he cannot paint anything but her face (“O Mimì tu più non torni”), then fools about with the others until Musetta brings Mimì in. He sells Musetta's earrings, brings back medicine, has seen the doctor and hurried him along, and is the one who embraces Rodolfo when Mimì has died.
[A-musetta-1] Musetta · Act 2: She arrives at the Café Momus with her elderly admirer Alcindoro, makes a scene to catch Marcello's eye, and sings him her waltz (“Quando me'n vo'”). Pretending her shoe hurts, she sends Alcindoro off for a new pair, falls into Marcello's arms, leaves Alcindoro both bills, and is carried off behind the band.
[A-musetta-2] Musetta · Act 3: Her voice is heard from the tavern at dawn, singing that pleasure is in the glass and love on young lips; Marcello says she teaches singing to the travellers. Caught flirting by Marcello, she insists on her freedom and storms off after a shouting match.
[A-musetta-3] Musetta · Act 4: Finding Mimì dying in the street, she brings her back to Rodolfo's garret, gives Marcello her earrings to sell for medicine, and buys Mimì the muff she longs for. While the medicine warms she prays to the Madonna to spare Mimì.
[A-schaunard-1] Schaunard · Act 1: He comes home in triumph with money, food and firewood, earned from an English lord by poisoning a parrot on the first floor with parsley, and decides the friends will drink at home and dine out. He helps keep the landlord's glass filled, then proposes the Café Momus.
[A-schaunard-2] Schaunard · Act 2: He buys a horn and a pipe in the Christmas crowd, orders supper in style because there is a lady at the table, and enjoys the comedy of Musetta and Marcello, until his money turns out to be gone. He marches off behind the band blowing his horn.
[A-schaunard-3] Schaunard · Act 4: He brings home four rolls for a mock banquet and fights a mock duel with Colline. When Mimì is brought in, he sees she has not long to live, leaves the lovers alone, and is the first to see that she has died.
[A-colline-1] Colline · Act 1: The philosopher comes in frozen, angrily throwing down the bundle of books he could not pawn (no pledges are taken on Christmas Eve), warms himself at the fire of Rodolfo's play, joins in throwing out the landlord, and is told to get a shave before going out. He falls down the dark stairs on the way.
[A-colline-2] Colline · Act 2: He buys a second-hand coat, has the mender stitch it, stuffs its pockets with books, and finds a rare Runic grammar. At the Café Momus he admits Mimì to the company in mock Latin and watches Musetta's performance, preferring his pipe and a Greek text to a woman like Musetta. At the end he helps carry Musetta off behind the band.
[A-colline-3] Colline · Act 4: He brings a herring to the mock banquet and duels with Schaunard using the fire tongs. When Mimì is brought in dying, he takes off his old coat to pawn it for her (“Vecchia zimarra”), and comes back with the money too late.
[A-benoit-1] Benoit · Act 1: The landlord comes for the rent, accepts a glass or two, and is flattered into boasting of his flirtations; then the tenants pretend to be shocked at a married man's morals and throw him out with the rent still unpaid.
[A-alcindoro-1] Alcindoro · Act 2: Musetta's elderly admirer trails after her through the crowd, puffing and cross, and is embarrassed by her scenes at the Café Momus. Sent running for a new pair of shoes, he comes back to find her gone and the friends' bill waiting for him, and collapses into a chair.
[A-parpignol-1] Parpignol · Act 2: The toy seller's cries are heard in the distance, then he wheels his cart of toys into the square with the children dancing after him, and leads them away again.
[A-sergente-1] Sergente · Act 3: At dawn the sergeant has the toll gate opened for the day's traffic, shows Mimì the tavern where the painter works, and waves the travellers through.
[A-doganiere-1] Doganiere · Act 3: Woken by the street sweepers banging on the gate, the customs officer lets them in, announces the milk women, and checks a basket for duty: empty.
