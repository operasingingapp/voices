# Check 9: story, arcs, WHEN lines, cast (independent check against the Ricordi vocal score)

Scope: STORY, act summaries, every section's "what happens", CHARACTER ARCS, WHEN lines, CAST descriptions in app-boheme.md.
Method: every claim read against the sung text and printed stage directions, pp. 1-277 (page map used to navigate; disputed or load-bearing pages read from the page images, e.g. pp. 30, 69, 92, 140, 166, 208, 230, 256, 276).

Result: the text is accurate. Every event, object, place, time, speaker and order of events checked out against the score. One small internal contradiction found; nothing else that rises to a real problem.

## Problems

1. S27 "Vecchia zimarra" (what happens) — contradiction with the rest of the file
   - Says: "he will sell the coat, and Schaunard will leave the two alone."
   - Score: Colline tells the coat it must now "ascendere il sacro monte" (go up the holy mount, i.e. the Monte di Pietà, the pawnshop), p. 258; at p. 260 he only says "io... questo!" (additando il pastrano). The Act 4 summary ("before taking it to the pawnshop"), S27's own first sentence ("it must go to the pawnshop") and A-colline-3 ("to pawn it for her") all say pawn.
   - Fix: "he will pawn the coat, and Schaunard will leave the two alone."

## Unsure / minor (not counted as problems)

- S10: "fills its pockets with books, including a rare Runic grammar". The score has Colline distribute his books in the coat's pockets (p. 91), then arrive waving an old book, "Copia rara, anzi unica; la grammatica Runica!" (p. 94). The score does not say the grammar went into a pocket. Harmless; "fills its pockets with books and shows off a rare Runic grammar" would be exact.
- S11: "Mimì orders a custard, Schaunard tells the waiter to do it in style". Schaunard's "E gran sfarzo. C'è una dama!" (p. 109) is about the whole order, not the custard; "it" reads as the custard. A-schaunard-2 has it right ("orders supper in style"). Possible rewording: "Schaunard tells the waiter to do it all in style: there is a lady present."
- S21: "her little gold ring" for "quel cerchietto d'or" (p. 208). "Cerchietto" can be a ring or a bracelet; ring is a fair reading.
