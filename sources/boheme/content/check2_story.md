# Check 2: story, running order, arcs, WHEN lines and cast (against the Ricordi vocal score)

I checked every factual claim in STORY, the four act summaries, the RUNNING ORDER "what happens" texts, the CHARACTER ARCS, the WHEN lines and the CAST descriptions against the sung words and printed stage directions. Most of the text is accurate and in the right order. These are the problems.

## Problems

### Wrong

1. **Act 3 summary, bullet 1**: "Marcello, who now lives at the tavern with Musetta, painting signs and giving lessons for his keep."
   Score p. 180: MAR. "Musetta insegna il canto ai passeggieri, io pingo quei guerrieri sulla facciata." Musetta gives the singing lessons and Marcello paints the soldiers on the front of the inn. The draft gives Marcello both jobs, and it contradicts S18, which has it right. Suggested fix: "...with Musetta, she teaching singing and he painting the front of the inn for their keep."

2. **S19 "Marcello. Finalmente!"**: "Rodolfo says she is a flirt who makes eyes at a little viscount."
   Score p. 196: "Un moscardino di Viscontino le fa l'occhio di triglia." It is the little viscount who makes eyes at her ("le fa" = to her), and she then shows her ankle and leads him on ("Ella sgonnella e scopre la caviglia con un far promettente e lusinghier", pp. 196–197). Suggested fix: "...a flirt who leads on a little viscount who makes eyes at her."

3. **Act 4 summary, bullet 1**: "each claiming not to care that he has seen his old love riding in a carriage."
   Score pp. 223–226: Rodolfo has seen Musetta, Marcello's old love, in a coupé, and Marcello has seen Mimì, Rodolfo's old love, in a carriage. Each one reports seeing the other man's lover, and each pretends not to care about the news of his own. S23 has this right. Suggested fix: "each tells the other he has seen the other's old love riding in a carriage, and each pretends not to care."

4. **S17 "Sa dirmi, scusi"**: "the bells of a nearby convent ring for matins".
   Score p. 178: "dall'Ospizio Maria Teresa suona mattutino". The bells are those of the Maria Theresa hospice (Ospizio, a charitable home or infirmary), not a convent.

5. **Act 1 summary, bullet 3** ("She nearly faints, recovers, leaves...") and **Mimì arc, Act 1** ("knocks at her neighbour's door, nearly faints").
   Score p. 56: "(sviene e Rodolfo è appena a tempo di sorreggerla...)". She faints outright, and on p. 57 she comes round ("rinviene"). S5 has it right ("and faints").

6. **S2 "Legna!"** ("an English lord paid him to play until his neighbour's parrot died") and **Schaunard arc, Act 1** ("an English lord whose parrot he poisoned").
   Score p. 26: "e un pappagallo m'addita al primo pian". The Englishman points to a parrot on the first floor, and the score never says whose it is. The neighbour belongs to Murger's novel, and the arc gives the parrot to the lord. The two texts also contradict each other. Suggested fix for both: "a parrot on the first floor".

7. **Schaunard arc, Act 1**: "He helps empty the landlord's glass".
   Score p. 39: "(mentre fanno chiacchierare Benoit, gli riempiono il bicchiere tosto che l'ha vuotato)". The friends keep refilling Benoit's glass, and he drinks it. "Empty" says the opposite. Suggested fix: "keeps the landlord's glass filled".

8. **Rodolfo arc, Act 1**: "joins the feast Schaunard's money buys".
   Score p. 30: Schaunard stops them eating ("impedisce agli amici di mangiarlo") and locks the food in the cupboard, and they only drink at home ("si beva in casa, ma si pranzi fuor", p. 32). No feast is eaten in the garret. Suggested fix: "shares the wine Schaunard's money buys".

### Not supported by the score

9. **CAST, Alcindoro**: "A rich, elderly admirer who pays for Musetta's dresses."
   Nothing in the score says he pays for her dresses. P. 116 describes him only as "un vecchio signore pomposo, pieno di pretensione negli abiti, nei modi, nella persona", and he pays for supper. Suggested fix: "Musetta's rich, pompous elderly admirer."

10. **CAST, Musetta**: "a flirt who lives off rich admirers but always comes back to him."
    "Always comes back to him" is not in the score. She leaves him in Act 3 with insults (p. 220), and in Act 4 she comes for Mimì's sake, not to return to Marcello. Suggested fix: "Marcello's old flame, a flirt who lives off rich admirers but still loves him."

11. **STORY**: "Months later, back in the garret..."
    The score gives no interval for Act 4 (p. 223: "IN SOFFITTA (Come nel Quadro Primo)"). Open question 25 already admits that "some time later" is the draft's own wording, so STORY should use the same words: "Some time later".

### Inaccurate or unclear wording

12. **S9 "O soave fanciulla"**: "she will stay close to him, and as for afterwards, he will see."
    Score p. 81: ROD. "E al ritorno?" MIMÌ (maliziosa) "Curioso!" He asks what happens when they come back, and she teases him for being nosy. "He will see" is a free invention. Suggested fix: "and when he asks about afterwards, she teases him for his curiosity."

13. **Act 1 summary, bullet 1** ("Rodolfo feeds it his play, one act at a time") and **S1** ("burns his play, one act at a time").
    Score pp. 12–17: he burns Act 1, then Act 2, then the remaining three acts at once ("Tre atti or voglio d'un colpo udir", p. 17, with the stage direction "getta sul fuoco il rimanente dello scartafaccio"). Suggested fix: "first one act, then another, then the rest at once".

14. **S25 "Che ora sia?"**: "with water in Colline's hat for champagne".
    Score p. 233: Schaunard "pone il cappello di Colline sul tavolo e vi colloca dentro una bottiglia d'acqua", while singing "Or lo Sciampagna mettiamo in ghiaccio". The water bottle is the champagne and the hat is the ice bucket. As written, the line suggests the hat is full of water. Suggested fix: "with a bottle of water in Colline's hat as champagne on ice".

15. **S1** ("their own dead stove") and **Act 1 summary** ("the stove is empty").
    The score has a fireplace: "camino" and "caminetto" (pp. 1, 4–5; "additando il camino senza fuoco"). "Stove" is the wrong object. Suggested fix: "fireplace" or "hearth".

16. **S1**: "As the last page burns out, the critics boo the author."
    No critics appear in the score. Marcello and Colline mock-boo ("Abbasso, abbasso l'autor!", pp. 19–20) as the fire dies out. A reader may think other people are present. Suggested fix: "Marcello and Colline boo the author."

## Unsure (worth a look, not clearly wrong)

- **Act 1 summary and Mimì arc, Act 1**, "her candle has gone out on the stairs": the score says only "mi s'è spento il lume" (p. 55) and does not say where. Her breathlessness is from the stairs ("Quelle scale...", p. 56).
- **Act 1 summary and Colline arc, Act 1**, "a pawnshop shut for the holiday": the score says "In giorno di Vigilia non s'accettano pegni" (p. 14). The pawnshop takes no pledges on Christmas Eve, which is not quite the same as being shut. S1's wording ("take nothing on Christmas Eve") is the exact one.
- **S21**, "her gold bracelet": the score has "quel cerchietto d'or" (p. 208), a little gold circlet, which can mean a bracelet or a ring. "Bracelet" is the usual reading. I did not check Castel's gloss.
- **Musetta arc, Act 3**, "teaching the customers a drinking song": in S16 she is only heard singing from the tavern (p. 172). "Teaching" is inferred from Marcello's later line on p. 180. It is acceptable, but it is an inference.
- **Alcindoro arc**, "laden and out of breath": the score has "(trafelato)" and his line "Come un facchino... correr di qua... di là" (p. 117). He compares himself to a porter, but no parcels are mentioned.
- **S8**, "keep two places at the Momus": the score has "Non son solo. Siamo in due. Andate da Momus, tenete il posto" (p. 76), so "keep a place" or "keep our places". It is close enough, but not literal.
- **S15**, "leaves the total on Alcindoro's seat": the score says "sul tavolo al posto d'Alcindoro" (p. 153), on the table at his place.
- **S3**, "Marcello closes it: he has paid the rent.": in the score this is Marcello's joke ("Ho pagato il trimestre!", p. 48). Read flatly, it could suggest that Marcello really paid. The Benoit arc ("without paying") has it right.
- **CAST, Benoit and Alcindoro**, "Sometimes sung by a tenor; often doubled": this is performance practice and Castel's information, not anything in this score. It is fine if that is intended.
- **Rodolfo arc, Act 4**, "Mimì has a rich protector": this is inferred from "in carrozza vestita come una regina" (p. 226) and Musetta's "Viscontino" (p. 249). It is reasonable.
