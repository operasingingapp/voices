# Check 8: story, act summaries, "what happens", arcs, WHEN lines, cast (app-boheme.md)

Method: independent pass. Every claim in STORY, the four act summaries, S1-S30 "what happens", CHARACTER ARCS, WHEN lines and CAST was compared with the sung text and printed stage directions. The page map was used only to find places. Pages viewed directly in the score images: 49, 69, 70, 83, 90, 92, 230. Castel was used for the Italian on Act 2 (book pp. 45-49).

Result: 2 problems (1 real, 1 minor). Everything else is supported by the score: who sings what, the objects, the order of events, places and times, and the quoted Italian (p. 69 really prints "Si." with no accent, as W-2 says; p. 230 has Rodolfo take the bonnet "dal cassetto del tavolo" while singing that she hid it "sotto il guancial", so S24 is consistent).

## Problems

1. Colline's coat in Act 2 is not "his old coat mended".
   - Where: Act 2 summary, bullet 1 ("Colline has his old coat mended and finds a rare book"); S10 ("Colline has his old coat mended and fills its pockets with books"); A-colline-2 ("He has his old coat mended, stuffs its pockets with books").
   - Score p. 90 sys 2: "(presso la rappezzatrice che gli ha cucito la falda di un zimarrone)", that is, "at the mender's, who has sewn the tail of a big coat for him". Colline sings "È un poco usato... ma è serio e a buon mercato" ("It's a bit worn... but it's sober and cheap"). Then on p. 91: "(paga, poi distribuisce ... i libri ... nelle molte tasche del zimarrone)". He is paying for a second-hand coat that he has just bought, and the mender has altered it. Castel (p. 45) reads it the same way: "mending the lapel of a big coat he has just bought". The coat becomes his "vecchia zimarra" only in Act 4.
   - Fix: "Colline buys a second-hand greatcoat (a bit worn, but sober and cheap), has the mender stitch it, and fills its many pockets with books, including a rare Runic grammar." In the act summary: "Colline buys a second-hand coat and finds a rare book". In A-colline-2: "He buys a cheap second-hand coat, stuffs its pockets with books ...".

2. (Minor, order) Act 2 summary, bullet 3: "As a military tattoo marches through the square Musetta has the two bills put together".
   - Score: Musetta has the bills added up and puts them at Alcindoro's place on pp. 148-153. At that point the tattoo is still far off and approaching ("La Ritirata - è lontanissima e andrà sempre avvicinandosi", p. 146). The tattoo only enters, behind the drum major, on p. 158. S15 has the order right ("As the military tattoo draws nearer").
   - Fix: "As a military tattoo approaches, Musetta has the two bills put together ...".

## Unsure (not counted as problems)

- S7, "She embroiders lilies and roses at home or out". On p. 70 "in casa e fuori" goes with "A tela o a seta ricamo" (she embroiders on linen or silk, at home or out), and "far gigli e rose" is her "svago" (pastime, delight). The merge is a fair paraphrase, but a literal version would be: "She embroiders on linen or silk, at home or out, and her delight is making lilies and roses".
- CAST, chorus groups for Act 2: "La folla: Studenti, Sartine, Borghesi, Bottegaie". On p. 83 the print says "La folla è composta di studenti, sartine, borghesi e popolo". Bottegaie is its own label (p. 116), and on pp. 155/158 it appears as "Borghesi, Bottegaie, ecc.". This is defensible, but "popolo" would follow p. 83 more closely.
- A-colline-2, "preferring his pipe and a Greek text to any woman". The text (p. 137-139) is "Essa è bella, non son cieco, ma piaccionmi assai più una pipa e un testo greco": he prefers them to her, Musetta. "to any woman" stretches it a little.
- A-mimi-4: "Left alone with him, she remembers ... takes Musetta's muff ... and falls asleep". The others have come back before she takes the muff (p. 271). The sentence reads as if she is still alone. Could be "Left alone with him, she remembers their first night; when the others return she takes the muff ...".
- Act 3 summary, bullet 2: "Marcello runs back inside when he hears Musetta laughing inside". The second "inside" is redundant.
- A-marcello-4, "sends for the doctor". On p. 271 Marcello says "Ho veduto il dottore! Verrà; gli ho fatto fretta" (he has seen the doctor and hurried him). "fetches the doctor" or "has seen the doctor" would be closer, but the current wording is not really wrong.
