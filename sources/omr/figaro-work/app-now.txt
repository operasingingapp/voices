SCENES
 Act 1 | Sinfonia | Overture |  | The overture, a whirl of quick, quiet strings bursting into full orchestra: the ‘mad day’ in miniature. It quotes no tune from the opera.
 Act 1 | Cinque… dieci… venti… | Duettino | figaro,susanna | Figaro measures the room for the marriage bed while Susanna tries on her bridal hat.
 Act 1 | Se a caso madama | Duettino | figaro,susanna | Susanna explains why the Count has given them a room so close to his own: he wants her.
 Act 1 | Bravo, signor padrone… Se vuol ballare | Recitative and cavatina | figaro | Alone, Figaro vows that if the Count wants to dance, he will call the tune.
 Act 1 | La vendetta | Aria | bartolo | Bartolo agrees to help Marcellina hold Figaro to his promise of marriage, and relishes revenge by law.
 Act 1 | Via resti servita | Duettino | susanna | Susanna and Marcellina trade elaborately polite insults in the doorway.
 Act 1 | Non so più cosa son, cosa faccio | Aria | cherubino | Cherubino, dismissed by the Count, has begged Susanna for help; he pours out his confusion about love.
 Act 1 | Cosa sento! | Trio | count,susanna | The Count, hidden, hears Basilio's gossip about Cherubino and the Countess, bursts out, and finds the page hiding in the armchair.
 Act 1 | Giovani liete | Chorus and recitative | figaro,susanna,count,cherubino | The villagers thank the Count for giving up his old right over his servants' brides; he puts the wedding off and sends Cherubino to the army.
 Act 1 | Non più andrai | Aria | figaro | Figaro teases Cherubino about the end of his life as a butterfly in the boudoir and the glories of army life.
 Act 2 | Porgi, amor | Cavatina | countess | Alone, the Countess asks love to give her back her husband's heart, or let her die.
 Act 2 | Figaro's plan | Recitative | countess,susanna,figaro | Figaro explains: an anonymous note to make the Count jealous, and Cherubino dressed as Susanna to keep her rendezvous.
 Act 2 | Voi che sapete | Arietta | cherubino | Cherubino sings the Countess the love song he has written for her.
 Act 2 | Venite, inginocchiatevi | Aria | susanna | Susanna dresses Cherubino as a girl, and admires the result.
 Act 2 | Quante buffonerie!… Che novità! | Recitative | countess,susanna,cherubino,count | Cherubino shows the women his commission. The Count knocks; Cherubino hides in the closet, and the Count, finding the door locked and hearing a noise, grows suspicious.
 Act 2 | Susanna, or via, sortite | Trio | count,countess,susanna | The Count, hearing a noise in the closet, orders ‘Susanna’ to come out; the Countess forbids it, and Susanna watches from hiding.
 Act 2 | Aprite, presto, aprite | Duettino | susanna,cherubino | While the Count fetches tools, Susanna takes Cherubino's place in the closet, and he jumps from the window.
 Act 2 | Esci omai, garzon malnato | Finale | count,countess,susanna,figaro,bartolo | The Countess confesses it is Cherubino inside; out steps Susanna, and the Count must beg pardon. Antonio complains of a man jumping onto his flowers, Figaro claims it was him and bluffs through Cherubino's dropped commission, and Marcellina, Bartolo and Basilio arrive with her claim.
 Act 3 | Crudel! perché finora | Duet | count,susanna | Sent by the Countess, Susanna promises the Count a meeting in the garden, muddling her yeses and noes.
 Act 3 | E perché fosti meco | Recitative | count,susanna,figaro | The Count asks why Susanna was so cold that morning. Leaving, she whispers to Figaro that his case is won, and the Count overhears.
 Act 3 | Hai già vinta la causa!… Vedrò mentr'io sospiro | Recitative and aria | count | Overhearing Susanna tell Figaro ‘the case is won’, the Count swears his servant will not have what he cannot.
 Act 3 | Riconosci in questo amplesso | Sextet | figaro,bartolo,count,susanna | A birthmark proves Figaro is Marcellina and Bartolo's lost son. Susanna arrives, sees him embracing Marcellina, slaps him, then learns the truth; the Count fumes.
 Act 3 | Andiam, andiam, bel paggio | Recitative | cherubino | Barbarina takes Cherubino off to her house to dress him as a girl among the village girls.
 Act 3 | E Susanna non vien! | Recitative | countess | Waiting for Susanna, the Countess worries their plan is too bold, then turns bitter at being brought so low by her husband.
 Act 3 | Dove sono | Aria | countess | She remembers the happy days of her marriage, and hopes her constancy may yet change her husband's heart.
 Act 3 | Io vi dico, signor | Recitative | count | The gardener Antonio tells the Count that Cherubino has not gone to Seville: his hat is at Barbarina's.
 Act 3 | Sull'aria | Duettino | countess,susanna | The Countess dictates, and Susanna writes, the note that sets the time and place of the garden meeting, sealed with a pin.
 Act 3 | Ricevete, o padroncina | Chorus and recitative | countess,susanna,cherubino,count | Village girls bring the Countess flowers; Cherubino, among them in a dress, is unmasked, and Barbarina asks the Count for him as her husband.
 Act 3 | Ecco la marcia | Finale | count,countess,susanna,figaro | A march brings in the two couples for the double wedding, Figaro and Susanna, Marcellina and Bartolo. During the fandango Susanna slips the note to the Count, who pricks his finger on the pin, and Figaro, seeing him, laughs at some lover's note. The Count invites everyone to the evening's celebrations.
 Act 4 | L'ho perduta, me meschina | Cavatina and recitative | figaro | Barbarina has lost the pin she was to return to Susanna; from her, Figaro learns of the rendezvous and thinks himself betrayed. Marcellina resolves to warn Susanna.
 Act 4 | Il capro e la capretta | Aria · often cut |  | Marcellina, now on Susanna's side, sings that only humans mistreat their mates.
 Act 4 | Nel padiglione a manca | Recitative | figaro,bartolo | In the dark garden Figaro posts Basilio and Bartolo nearby, to come when he whistles and witness the betrayal.
 Act 4 | In quegli anni | Aria · often cut |  | Basilio explains how a donkey's skin once saved him: give way to the powerful.
 Act 4 | Tutto è disposto… Aprite un po' quegli occhi | Recitative and aria | figaro | Hiding in the dark, Figaro warns all men to open their eyes to women.
 Act 4 | Signora, ella mi disse | Recitative | susanna,countess | Marcellina tells Susanna that Figaro is hiding nearby; Susanna and the Countess, in each other's clothes, take their places.
 Act 4 | Giunse alfin il momento… Deh vieni, non tardar | Recitative and aria | susanna | In the Countess's clothes, knowing Figaro is listening, Susanna sings of her longing for her lover.
 Act 4 | Pian pianin le andrò più presso | Finale | cherubino,countess,count,figaro,susanna | In the dark Cherubino flirts with the disguised Countess, the Count woos his own wife, and Figaro courts Susanna as ‘the Countess’ until they make up. The Count calls everyone and refuses pardon, until his true wife appears; he kneels (“Contessa, perdono”), she forgives, and all celebrate.
