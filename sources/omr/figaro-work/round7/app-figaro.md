# STORY (shown on the opera's page)
On their wedding day, Susanna tells Figaro that their master, Count Almaviva, wants her for himself. With the neglected Countess they plot to catch him out, while the Count does all he can to put off the wedding, the lovesick page Cherubino keeps turning up where he shouldn't, and old Marcellina tries to hold Figaro to a promise of marriage, until a mark on his arm shows she is his long-lost mother and the two couples marry together. As bait, Susanna promises the Count a meeting in the garden that night, but the Countess will go in her place. Figaro, not in on the plan, thinks Susanna is betraying him and lies in wait. In the dark the Count is caught courting his own wife, and he begs her forgiveness.

# CAST (name, voice type, one-line description)
- [C-figaro] Figaro (Bass-baritone): The Count's valet, about to marry Susanna. (Mozart's score lists the role as a bass; today it is usually sung by a bass-baritone.)
- [C-susanna] Susanna (Soprano): The Countess's maid, about to marry Figaro.
- [C-countess] The Countess (Soprano): Rosina of Beaumarchais's play The Barber of Seville, now Countess Almaviva, neglected by her husband.
- [C-count] Count Almaviva (Baritone): The Countess's husband, jealous of his wife though unfaithful himself, who has given up his feudal right over his servants' brides and would like it back for Susanna. (Mozart's score lists the role as a bass; today it is sung by a baritone.)
- [C-cherubino] Cherubino (Mezzo-soprano): The Count's young page, in love with every woman he sees; a trouser role, sung by a woman. (Mozart wrote the role for a soprano; today it is usually sung by a mezzo.)
- [C-bartolo] Doctor Bartolo (Bass): A doctor from Seville, Rosina's old guardian, who has not forgiven Figaro for helping her marry the Count.
- [C-marcellina] Marcellina (Mezzo-soprano): Housekeeper at the castle, once Bartolo's servant, who has lent Figaro money on his promise to marry her. (Mozart wrote the role for a soprano; today it is usually sung by a mezzo.)
- [C-basilio] Don Basilio (Tenor): The music master, a gossip in the Count's pay.
- [C-curzio] Don Curzio (Tenor): The judge who hears Marcellina's case against Figaro.
- [C-barbarina] Barbarina (Soprano): The gardener's young daughter, Susanna's cousin, sweet on Cherubino; the Count has been flirting with her.
- [C-antonio] Antonio (Bass): The Count's gardener, Susanna's uncle and Barbarina's father.

The Count knocks at the locked door; Cherubino hides in the closet. Antonio the gardener complains of a man jumping onto his carnations; Figaro claims it was him; Antonio thought it looked like the boy, but Figaro sticks to his story, rubbing his foot as if he had hurt it in the jump, and, prompted by the women, explains Cherubino's dropped commission; the Count tears it up in fury. Susanna asks the Count, on the Countess's behalf, for his smelling-salts flask (a pretext to speak to him), and hints that she could pay off Marcellina with the dowry he promised her.

    As a march approaches, the Count, left alone with his wife, fumes; the Countess, frozen with alarm, cuts him short: they must sit and receive the two couples. He sits, plotting revenge. The march brings in the couples for the double wedding; Bartolo leads Susanna to the Count and Figaro leads Marcellina to the Countess to receive their bridal hats, while two girls and the chorus praise the Count for giving up his right. Kneeling, Susanna slips him the note; he pricks his finger on the pin, reads the note, and keeps the pin on his sleeve, while Figaro, watching, laughs at some lover's note. The Count invites everyone to the evening's celebrations.
[S48] Act 4 | L'ho perduta, me meschina | Cavatina and recitative | sings: barbarina, figaro, marcellina | on stage: -
    In a small room, Barbarina has lost the pin that sealed the love note Susanna slipped the Count at the wedding, which the Count told her to return to Susanna as his answer: ‘the seal of the pines’. Slipping her a pin from Marcellina's dress, Figaro gets the message out of her, works out that the meeting is under the pines, and thinks Susanna has betrayed him. Marcellina tells him to keep calm, but he storms off to avenge all husbands.

    Marcellina resolves to warn Susanna, whom she believes innocent: when her own interest is not at stake, every woman is inclined to defend her sex against ungrateful men. Animals live at peace with their mates; only women are treated cruelly by men.
[S50] Act 4 | Nel padiglione a manca | Recitative | sings: barbarina | on stage: -
    Alone in the dark garden with an orange, a pear and a ring-cake she has begged (at the price of a kiss) for Cherubino, Barbarina looks for the pavilion on the left, and hides in it when someone comes.
[S51] Act 4 | È Barbarina… Chi va là? | Recitative | sings: figaro, basilio, bartolo | on stage: barbarina
    Figaro, in a cloak with a lantern, meets Basilio, Bartolo and a troop of workmen he has summoned to the garden, and tells them to stay close and come running when he whistles, to witness the betrayal.
[S52] Act 4 | Ha i diavoli nel corpo | Recitative | sings: basilio, bartolo | on stage: barbarina
    Basilio explains that the Count wants Susanna and she has agreed to meet him: why should Figaro mind what so many others put up with? It never pays to quarrel with the great.
[S53] Act 4 | In quegli anni | Aria · often cut | sings: basilio | on stage: bartolo, barbarina
    Basilio tells how he was once a hot-headed youth, until time and dangers brought him Lady Calm (‘donna flemma’), who gave him a donkey's skin. Caught in a storm he wrapped himself in it; when the storm passed, a wild beast came at him and turned away in disgust at the smell. Shame, danger and death can be escaped under a donkey's hide.
[S54] Act 4 | Tutto è disposto… Aprite un po' quegli occhi | Recitative and aria | sings: figaro | on stage: barbarina
    Alone in the dark, waiting to catch Susanna with the Count, Figaro realises that at the wedding the Count was enjoying her note while he, watching, unknowingly laughed at himself. He warns all men to open their eyes and see what women really are: witches, sirens, owls, comets, vixens.
[S55] Act 4 | Signora, ella mi disse | Recitative | sings: susanna, marcellina, countess, figaro | on stage: barbarina
    Susanna and the Countess come in each other's clothes. Marcellina warns them that Figaro is here: he has learned of the pin and thinks Susanna is going to meet the Count. She hides. Figaro watches from hiding, muttering. The Countess, saying the night feels damp, withdraws and hides; Susanna, knowing Figaro is listening, says she will stay and take the fresh air under the trees.
[S56] Act 4 | Giunse alfin il momento… Deh vieni, non tardar | Recitative and aria | sings: susanna | on stage: figaro, countess, barbarina, marcellina
    Knowing Figaro can hear her, and is jealous, Susanna sings of her longing for her lover: come, do not delay, my joy, in the night's quiet, and let me crown you with roses.
[S57] Act 4 | Perfida! E in quella forma | Recitative | sings: figaro, cherubino, countess | on stage: susanna, barbarina, marcellina
    Figaro, sure now that Susanna is false, can hardly believe his ears. Cherubino comes along humming, heading for the pavilion where Barbarina is; he sees a woman in the shadows and, by her hat, takes her for Susanna. It is the Countess, who dreads the Count arriving now.
[S58] Act 4 | Pian pianin le andrò più presso | Finale | sings: cherubino, countess, count, figaro, susanna, antonio, basilio, bartolo, curzio, barbarina, marcellina | on stage: -
    In the dark Cherubino flirts with the disguised Countess, thinking her Susanna, until the Count steps between them and takes the kiss; his slap lands on Figaro. The Count woos his own wife and gives her a ring. Figaro takes the disguised Susanna for the Countess and tells her that her husband is with his bride; then he knows her by her voice and pretends to court ‘the Countess’, until she slaps him and they make up. She tells him the Count has been wooing his own wife, and together they play a love scene for the Count to overhear. Believing his wife unfaithful, the Count calls everyone with torches and drags Cherubino, Barbarina, Marcellina and ‘the Countess’ out of the pavilion; the real Countess has slipped away into the other one. He refuses pardon though all kneel, until his true wife appears; he begs her forgiveness (‘Contessa, perdono’), she forgives, and all celebrate.

# CHARACTER ARCS (act by act)
[A-figaro-1] Figaro · Act 1: On his wedding day Figaro measures the new room, until Susanna tells him why the Count wants them so close at hand. He vows to make the Count dance to his tune (“Se vuol ballare”), brings the villagers to press for the wedding, and teases Cherubino about army life (“Non più andrai”), after quietly telling him they must talk before he leaves.
[A-figaro-2] Figaro · Act 2: Having kept Cherubino from leaving, he plots with the women: an anonymous note to make the Count jealous, and Cherubino in a dress to keep Susanna's rendezvous. Shown the note, he denies writing it. When the gardener complains of a man jumping from the window, Figaro claims it was him and bluffs through the dropped commission. Marcellina then arrives to claim him.
[A-figaro-3] Figaro · Act 3: Ordered to pay Marcellina or marry her, he is saved by the mark on his arm: she and Bartolo are his long-lost parents. Susanna slaps him before she understands; then the family celebrates, Marcellina hands back his IOU as his dowry, and a double wedding is set for today. Confronted with Cherubino, he bluffs that they may both have jumped, At the wedding, where he leads Marcellina to the Countess while Bartolo leads Susanna to the Count, he sees the Count prick his finger on some lover's note.
[A-figaro-4] Figaro · Act 4: Learning from Barbarina that the pin-sealed note he saw the Count receive came from Susanna, he believes she is betraying him. He brings Bartolo, Basilio and the workmen to the garden to come running at his whistle and witness it, and rails against women (“Aprite un po' quegli occhi”). After taking the slap the Count meant for Cherubino, he tells ‘the Countess’ that her husband is with his bride, then recognises Susanna's voice under the Countess's clothes, pays her back by courting her, and they make up; only now does he learn of the women's plan, and they play a love scene to bait the Count, until the Count is caught out.
[A-susanna-1] Susanna · Act 1: Susanna warns Figaro that the Count wants her and that Basilio pleads his case, trades sugary insults with Marcellina at the door (“Via resti servita”), and fends the Count off. She hides Cherubino in the armchair, but he is discovered anyway.
[A-susanna-2] Susanna · Act 2: With the Countess she starts dressing Cherubino as a girl (“Venite, inginocchiatevi”). When the Count, suspecting a lover in the locked closet, goes off for tools to break it open, she lets the page out, sees him jump from the window, and takes his place, stepping out to the Count's confusion. In the finale she joins the Countess in reproaching the Count, admits the note was Figaro's, and prompts Figaro through the matter of the commission.
[A-susanna-3] Susanna · Act 3: Sent by the Countess, she promises the Count a meeting in the garden (the Countess will go in her place, in her clothes, and Figaro must not know), hinting she will pay off Marcellina with the dowry he promised her, then whispers to Figaro that he has won his case without a lawyer, and the Count overhears. She comes to the trial with money to buy Figaro free. Finding Figaro embracing Marcellina, she slaps him, then learns Marcellina is his mother. She writes the note setting the rendezvous to the Countess's dictation (“Sull'aria”) and slips it to the Count at the wedding.
[A-susanna-4] Susanna · Act 4: Figaro has learned of the pin and thinks she means to meet the Count. Wearing the Countess's clothes, and warned by Marcellina that Figaro is hiding nearby, she knows he is listening and teases him with a song of longing (“Deh vieni, non tardar”). When he courts her as ‘the Countess’ she boxes his ears, then forgives him, and together they play out a love scene to bait the Count. When the Count drags everyone out of hiding, she comes out in the Countess's clothes and begs his pardon, until the real Countess appears.
[A-countess-1] The Countess · Act 2: Alone, the Countess grieves for her husband's lost love (“Porgi, amor”) and agrees to Figaro's plan. Dressing Cherubino, she takes back her ribbon from his arm and dries his tears. When the Count finds the closet locked she has to confess Cherubino is inside, only for Susanna to step out; the Count begs her pardon, and after reproaching him she grants it.
[A-countess-2] The Countess · Act 3: She sends Susanna to promise the Count a meeting in the garden, meaning to go herself in Susanna's clothes, without telling Figaro. Waiting, she remembers the happy days of her marriage and hopes to win her husband back (“E Susanna non vien!”, “Dove sono”). She dictates the note that sets the time and place (“Sull'aria”), sealed with one of her pins, and welcomes the village girls, among them Cherubino in disguise; when he is unmasked she has to face the Count's anger.
[A-countess-3] The Countess · Act 4: In Susanna's clothes she fends off Cherubino and is wooed by her own husband, who gives her a ring, then slips away into the pavilion on the right. When the Count refuses all pardon, she steps forward as herself and tries to kneel for the others; he stops her and begs her forgiveness, and she forgives him.
[A-count-1] Count Almaviva · Act 1: The Count tries to make a date with Susanna, hides from Basilio behind the armchair, and finds Cherubino on it. When Figaro brings the villagers to thank him for giving up his feudal right, he promises the wedding but asks for a delay, and sends the page, who has overheard him courting Susanna, to his regiment.
[A-count-2] Count Almaviva · Act 2: An anonymous note makes him jealous. Hearing a noise in the locked closet, he fetches tools to break it open; his wife confesses Cherubino is inside, but out steps Susanna, and he has to beg her pardon. He questions Figaro over the note, Antonio's crushed carnations and the page's commission, and welcomes Marcellina's claim on Figaro.
[A-count-3] Count Almaviva · Act 3: Susanna agrees to meet him, but he overhears her telling Figaro he has won his case, and swears he will not lose to his servant (“Vedrò mentr'io sospiro”). The trial backfires when Figaro proves to be Marcellina's son. Antonio tells him Cherubino is still in the castle, and later unmasks the page among the village girls; and Barbarina embarrasses him by asking for the page as her husband. He sits down to the wedding brooding on revenge, receives Susanna's note, pricks his finger on its pin and keeps it, to send back through Barbarina as his answer.
[A-count-4] Count Almaviva · Act 4: In the garden Cherubino's kiss meant for ‘Susanna’ lands on him, and his slap meant for the page lands on Figaro. He courts ‘Susanna’, really his wife, and gives her a ring. Finding Figaro with ‘the Countess’, he calls everyone and refuses pardon though all kneel to him, until his true wife appears, and he begs her forgiveness (‘Contessa, perdono’).
[A-cherubino-1] Cherubino · Act 1: Dismissed for being caught with Barbarina, the page begs Susanna to help him stay, snatches the Countess's ribbon from her, and pours out his confusion about love (“Non so più cosa son”). Hiding behind the armchair, then in it, he overhears the Count courting Susanna; discovered, he is packed off to the army, all the more quickly for what he heard.
[A-cherubino-2] Cherubino · Act 2: He sings the Countess his love song (“Voi che sapete”) and is partly dressed as a girl. She finds her ribbon round his scratched arm and keeps it, and dries his tears when he nearly declares himself. He hides in the closet when the Count arrives, and escapes by jumping from the window.
[A-cherubino-3] Cherubino · Act 3: Supposed to have left for Seville, he is dressed as a girl at Barbarina's house. Disguised among the village girls bringing flowers, he is found out, and Barbarina puts the Count on the spot by asking for him as her husband.
[A-cherubino-4] Cherubino · Act 4: In the dark garden, heading for the pavilion where Barbarina is waiting, he finds ‘Susanna’, really the Countess, flirts with her, and tries to steal a kiss, taunting her that he was behind the armchair this morning; the kiss lands on the Count. He escapes into the pavilion, and the Count's slap lands on Figaro. At the end the Count drags him out, the first of those hiding there.
[A-bartolo-1] Doctor Bartolo · Act 1: Doctor Bartolo still resents Figaro for helping Rosina marry the Count, and promises Marcellina revenge by law (“La vendetta”).
[A-bartolo-2] Doctor Bartolo · Act 2: He arrives with Marcellina and Basilio to press her claim on Figaro.
[A-bartolo-3] Doctor Bartolo · Act 3: At the hearing the mark on Figaro's arm reveals that he is his and Marcellina's son, stolen as a baby. He agrees to marry Marcellina at last, and throws Figaro a purse of money as a wedding gift; at the double wedding he leads Susanna to the Count.
[A-bartolo-4] Doctor Bartolo · Act 4: In the garden at night Figaro posts him and Basilio nearby, to come when he whistles and witness Susanna's betrayal. He comes running with the others at the end and sees the Count beg his wife's pardon.
[A-marcellina-1] Marcellina · Act 1: Marcellina holds a contract: Figaro borrowed money from her and promised to marry her if he could not repay it. With Bartolo's help she means to enforce it, and she trades sugary insults with Susanna at the door (“Via resti servita”).
[A-marcellina-2] Marcellina · Act 2: At the end of the act she arrives with Bartolo and Basilio to lay her claim before the Count, just as the Count, out of excuses for delaying the wedding, is longing for her to arrive.
[A-marcellina-3] Marcellina · Act 3: The judge orders Figaro to pay or marry her, but a mark on his arm, which she names before seeing it, shows that he is her own long-lost son by Bartolo (“Riconosci in questo amplesso”). Mother and son embrace, and Bartolo agrees to marry her at last, so a double wedding is set for today. She gives Figaro back his IOU as his dowry, and at the wedding Figaro leads her to the Countess.
[A-marcellina-4] Marcellina · Act 4: When Figaro learns of the pin and thinks Susanna false, she tells him to keep calm (‘Flemma, flemma’). Now on Susanna's side, she resolves to warn her of Figaro's suspicions and reflects that animals live at peace with their mates, while only women are treated cruelly by men (“Il capro e la capretta”). In the dark garden she warns the women that Figaro is there and hides in the pavilion, where the Count later finds her with Barbarina and Cherubino.
[A-basilio-1] Don Basilio · Act 1: The music master brings the Count's advances to Susanna and spreads gossip about Cherubino and the Countess, not knowing the Count is hiding to hear it, and is caught out when the Count bursts from his hiding place (“Cosa sento!”). He is there when the villagers praise the Count and Cherubino is sent to the army.
[A-basilio-2] Don Basilio · Act 2: Having delivered Figaro's anonymous note to the Count, he arrives at the end of the act with Marcellina and Bartolo to support her claim.
[A-basilio-3] Don Basilio · Act 4: Called by Figaro to the garden to witness Susanna's betrayal, he explains how he learned to give way to the powerful (“In quegli anni”). He hides nearby and comes out with the others at the end.
[A-curzio-1] Don Curzio · Act 3: The judge rules that Figaro must pay Marcellina or marry her, and stays to see the case turned upside down when Figaro turns out to be her son (“Riconosci in questo amplesso”).
[A-curzio-2] Don Curzio · Act 4: In Mozart's score he is among those who come running with torches at the end, and sees the Count beg his wife's pardon.
[A-barbarina-1] Barbarina · Act 3: Sweet on Cherubino, she takes him home to dress him as a village girl, and brings him with the girls who give flowers to the Countess, passing him off as her cousin. When he is found out, she reminds the Count of the promises he made her when he kissed her, and asks for Cherubino as her husband.
[A-barbarina-2] Barbarina · Act 4: Sent by the Count to return to Susanna, as his answer, the pin that sealed the love note she slipped him at the wedding, she has lost it (“L'ho perduta, me meschina”), and lets slip the Count's message, ‘the seal of the pines’, which tells Figaro where the meeting will be. Then, in the dark garden, she waits with an orange, a pear and a ring-cake for Cherubino in the pavilion on the left, where the Count finds her at the end.
[A-antonio-1] Antonio · Act 2: The gardener storms in, furious (the others say drunk), complaining that a man has jumped from the window onto his flowers, and hands Figaro the papers the man dropped, which the Count takes: Cherubino's commission.
[A-antonio-2] Antonio · Act 3: He tells the Count that Cherubino has not gone to Seville, and later unmasks the page among the village girls by pulling off his bonnet and putting the page's own hat on his head.
[A-antonio-3] Antonio · Act 4: He comes with torches at the end, sees his daughter Barbarina come out of hiding after the page, and sees the Count beg his wife's pardon.

# CUSTOM PIECE CONTEXTS (replace the running-order text for that piece)
[P-countess] E Susanna non vien!: Alone, the Countess waits for Susanna, anxious to know how the Count took her proposal. The plan seems bold with so jealous a husband, but what harm is there in changing clothes with Susanna under cover of night, to catch him herself? Then she turns bitter: the Count who loved her, then wronged her, then betrayed her, has brought her so low that she must ask her own maid for help.
[P-countess] Dove sono: Still alone, the Countess remembers the happy days when the Count loved her and asks where his promises have gone. The slow, singing first part looks back; in the quick ending she clings to the hope that her faithfulness may yet win back his ungrateful heart.
